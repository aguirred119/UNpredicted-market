Price vs probability

A 60-cent YES quote reflects an available market price. It is not a certified 60% chance. Spreads, liquidity and fees affect trading outcomes.

What simulations do

Our binary simulation samples outcomes from an input probability. More trials reduce sampling noise; they do not make the input more accurate.

What AI does here

The lab trains logistic regression from historical examples. That is machine learning. Manual probability mode uses your assumption and has no AI involvement.

Sports research essentials

Which sports inputs belong in a model?

Inputs could include pregame ratings, known injuries, starting lineups, rest days or venue. Keep the definitions consistent and record when the information became available. Final scores, postgame statistics and closing prices unavailable at the forecast time can leak the answer into training.

Why do settlement rules matter?

A model trained on regulation wins may not match a market including overtime. Draws, postponements, player participation and canceled games need explicit handling. Always compare the same defined outcome.

Why use a chronological holdout?

We train on the earliest 80% and test on the latest 20%. Test labels never enter training. Repeatedly changing a model after seeing test results can still overfit the holdout.

What is a Brier score?

Average squared difference between predicted probability and binary outcome. Lower is better. Compare against a constant baseline trained on the earlier period. It measures probabilistic error, not trading profit.

Does a 70% forecast mean a 70% win rate?

No. It is a model estimate for a defined outcome. Calibration requires many comparable forecasts and observed outcomes. Sample size and changing conditions matter.

Is a model-price difference a profitable edge?

No. It may reflect model error, different settlement definitions, stale data, fees or spread. Our comparison omits execution costs and does not establish positive returns.

What should be recorded before publishing a pick?

Event definition, deadline, inputs available at publication, data source permissions, model version, probability, reproducible simulation settings and an independently preserved timestamp.
