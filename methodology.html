The process behind the probability.

Machine-learning classifier

The browser trains a two-feature logistic regression with standardized inputs, 600 full-batch gradient steps, learning rate 0.1 and L2 penalty 0.01 on feature weights. The earliest 80% of observations train the model. The remaining 20% are a chronological holdout. Standardization uses training data only.

The model is generic, experimental and not trained on an operator-supplied sports or market dataset. You supply the dataset and define the features. Synthetic demo results are labeled and are not evidence of real performance.

Simulation

Each trial samples an independent Bernoulli outcome from a fixed probability. A seeded Mulberry32 generator permits reproduction. The simulation report shows YES/NO counts and Monte Carlo standard error. This error describes sampling variation, not uncertainty about the real outcome probability. Repeating an arbitrary probability cannot validate it.

Limitations

Features may contain leakage; user confirmation does not detect it. Small samples, omitted variables, dependence, imbalance, selection effects and distribution change can invalidate a model. The lab does not implement confidence intervals for model parameters, automated feature discovery or execution-aware backtesting. Inputs outside the observed training range are flagged.

Evidence and results

Downloaded run records include timestamps, source labels, dataset hash where applicable, model parameters, holdout metrics, seed and trial counts. SHA-256 detects changes when compared with a trusted copy; it does not certify data truth or independently prove publication time. The local notebook is editable and cannot support a claim of audited public performance.

Advertising standard

We describe capabilities that actually exist. Manual runs are labeled assumptions. Trained runs are labeled machine-learning experiments. We do not claim guaranteed outcomes, demonstrated profitability or real-world accuracy from synthetic samples.
